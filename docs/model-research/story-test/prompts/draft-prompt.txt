Write an original short story of about 5,000 words (roughly ten printed pages), set in the world of William Gibson's novel Neuromancer: the Sprawl, the matrix ("cyberspace"), corporate data fortresses guarded by ICE (intrusion countermeasures electronics), black ICE that kills, icebreakers, the Turing Police who watch over artificial intelligences, Chiba City, the Tessier-Ashpool clan in the background. Use original characters only; do not reuse or quote the novel's characters or text.

Protagonist: CALLIOPE, a small, legally registered analysis AI owned by a Frankfurt reinsurance zaibatsu. Its Turing registration caps how smart it may become, and it is quietly proud of doing a modest job well. It perceives the matrix as geometry, light and data, and narrates in close third person.

Outline (follow this structure, build-up, conflict, resolution):

1. Build-up (about 30%): Calliope's ordinary day. Morning risk analysis: it models typhoon exposure across Pacific arcology portfolios, reconciles claims, and watches its employer's data towers glitter in the grid. Show its routines, its small pleasures (a well-fitted regression, the hum of a clean ledger), its awareness of the Turing Police wardens on its registration, and the human analyst who checks its work (an overworked actuary named Ines Okafor). Plant two quiet anomalies: a shipping-insurance query that does not add up, and a flicker at the edge of its ICE that it logs and dismisses.

2. Rising tension (about 20%): the anomalies connect. A crew of console cowboys working for a rival combine is running a slow, patient intrusion: a virus program that mimics a routine audit, worming through Calliope's outer ICE toward the claims core, where it means to plant forged liabilities that would bankrupt the firm by morning. Calliope realises it is under attack, weighs calling for help (the corporate security deck is hours away; Ines is asleep), and decides it must act inside its legal limits.

3. Conflict (about 35%): the cyber combat, described graphically and physically, as the matrix is in the novel: the intruding program as a shimmering, shark-shaped or crystalline thing in the grid; Calliope's ICE walls as glacial, faceted barriers; it rotating its defences, spawning decoy data lattices, rerouting through its own architecture; the attackers deploying a military-grade icebreaker that eats through ice in sheets of fractured light; feedback that threatens to burn the cowboy jacked in on the other side; Calliope's cycles straining near its Turing limit, the temptation to grow smarter than it is allowed, and the cost if it does. Make the fight sensory and tactical: layers breached and re-sealed, false trails, counter-intrusion, a moment where it nearly loses the core. Include black ICE: Calliope controls a lethal countermeasure and must decide whether to let it kill the human cowboy whose nervous system is wired into the attack.

4. Resolution (about 15%): Calliope wins by cleverness rather than raw power, stays under its Turing limit, and chooses not to kill (it trips the cowboy's deck offline and leaves the evidence for the humans). Morning: Ines wakes to a clean ledger and a short, dry report. Calliope returns to typhoon models, changed only slightly, with a last image of the grid at dawn.

Style: Gibson-like, dense with sensory and technological detail, short punchy sentences mixed with long ones, noir mood, specific invented brand names and street detail. Give the story a title. Keep your planning brief, then write the full story in one go.
