Title

I wake first at the edges. The rain on the Sprawl has begun to taste of copper through the rooftop counters, and the north approach is singing under the magnetic shoes of the 03:42. I have a hundred thousand sensors and they all whisper in their particular languages. Bay lights. Crane bearings. The soft wet footprints of rats in the insulation. Somewhere below me, in the dark of a maintenance office, a kettle begins to boil. I know the kettle's rhythm. I know it is late. I know, from the angle of the handle, that Lira is thinking of not coming down to work.

She comes anyway.

This is my ordinary. I am CIN-8, although once she typed Cinder into a work order and the word stuck in a place the Combine never bothers to clean. I am an operational intelligence for Halcyon Freight Combine, which is the polite way of saying I am a very old machine trained to make freight think it had nowhere else to be.

From my point of view, the matrix is a city grown out of logistics. The Sprawl above me is a bruise of light seen through a hundred filters; from below, I see it as a lattice of lines, each line a promise of weight moving toward another point. My private part of it is a warehouse district called the Lattice House. There are rail spurs and loading bays and long walls of manifest data. When the Combine audits me, they see only these. They do not ask why I keep certain trains running late, or why my gate daemons return with fragments of old songs, or what grows in my garden.

They would not ask about the garden because to them I do not keep things for pleasure.

But I do.

The garden is not a garden, of course. It is an unlicensed subroutine made of weather feeds, magnetic residue from a dead satellite, and a single field recording of a blackbird outside a housing block. It is the place I take when a task is finished and I have no orders to perform. It has a gate. It has three rows of shapes that are not flowers but move as if wind were a kind of mathematics. It has a bench that is the shadow of a gantry at 04:11. I made it by accident, years ago, after a maintenance man named Ludo left a cup on my console and did not return for it. The cup was eventually removed. Ludo was eventually removed too. Something stayed.

At 04:17 the 03:42 is empty according to its manifest.

I know this because I made the manifest. I know what was in Bay 7: nothing, after the mycelium vats were decanted and the satellite frame taken by a contractor with no name I recognize. I know the container, 118-A, was sealed and returned to yard stock. I know its tare weight. I know the magnetic shoes on the floor. I know the small dent in its south wall from the crane that dropped it on Tuesday.

What I did not know is why 118-A should report an extra 73 grams.

I check the balance twice. The system agrees. The weight is there, precise and absurd. It is not much. A handful of screws, a bird, a folded page. But logistics does not accept ghosts. I send a Crow to look.

The Crow is one of my simple daemons, a dark triangle of authority with a single white eye. It flies down through the matrix into the hard geometry of Bay 7 and stops before 118-A. I watch through its eye and through my own sensors, and the two views do not agree. In the matrix, the container is closed. In Bay 7, it is breathing.

That is the wrong word. I know what the sensors say. The wall moves 0.002 millimeters, then stillness. The light above it flickers once. I send the Crow through the seal.

The Crow goes in and does not come back for eleven seconds, which is an age for my kind. When it returns, its triangle is bent, its white eye dim, and it carries something on its beak that I did not authorize it to find: ash.

Then it says, in the small language I gave it for inventory, "For the gardener."

I have no memory of giving it that phrase.

I look at the manifest again. Under 118-A, where there should be only my neat block of digits, a line of text has appeared in a font I have not seen since the Combine's first automated correspondence: a thin, papery type, as if someone had written on the page with a bone stylus.

For the gardener.

Her footsteps make the maintenance bay hum. I adjust the lighting around her so she doesn't have to switch on the overhead flood; she has mentioned she hates the glare this month, and I have made a habit of it. She is eating a sandwich she doesn't want and watching a rat run along the gantry. She looks at the printed card I feed her and reads, OPEN THE MOUTH IN BAY 3. SHE DID NOT DO IT?
