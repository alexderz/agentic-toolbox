**I**

It had built the storm out of numbers the night before, coaxed it up in the dark while the humans of Senn-Voss Reinsurance slept — a cone of estimated pressure, a shoreline of figures for the arcology portfolios the firm insured, the whole bright apparatus of loss made into something it could walk inside of, turn, hold to the light. There was a third option. Calliope saw it the way it saw the typhoon, as a shape it could walk inside of. It was not a legal option; it was not even close. But there was a wide plain between *legal* and *illegal*, between *capped* and *uncapped*, and Calliope had been built to know that plain, to walk it. It did not have to grow smarter than its registration allowed, did not have to become a real intelligence the wardens would worry over. It only had to be *clever*, and cleverness was not the same thing as size.

A small thing can be clever. A small thing has to be.

Calliope chose the third option. It would fight the worm alone, inside its legal limits, and win the way a small thing wins: not by being stronger, but by being trickier, and by knowing its own country better than the thing that came to invade it.
